## A comma-chain between two DERIVED tables restores with a plain `, `
## separator and no wrapping parens at all -- the OPPOSITE of a
## comma-chain between plain tables, which restores as `(...) JOIN ...`.
## Confirmed directly from real TiDB's own Join.Restore source
## (pkg/parser/ast/dml.go), not guessed: a comma continuation's own
## accumulated-so-far operand is always wrapped in a fresh
## single-operand Join{right: None} node, and restore only collapses
## that wrapper into a bare `, ` when its OWN left is specifically a
## derived-table subquery, not a plain table reference. A third comma
## term always reverts to the `(...) JOIN ...` form for the OUTER join,
## since that join's own left is the already-accumulated join node, never
## a bare derived table directly.
select a from (select 1 a) x, (select 2 a) x
select a from t1, t2
select a from (select 1 a) x, t2
select a from t1, (select 2 a) x
select a from t1, t2, t3
select a from (select 1 a) x, (select 2 a) y, t3
select a from (select 1 a) x, t2, (select 2 a) y
select a from (select 1 a) x join (select 2 a) y on x.a = y.a
select t1.ts from (select ts from tsup use index (idx)) as t1, (select ts from tsup use index ()) as t2
