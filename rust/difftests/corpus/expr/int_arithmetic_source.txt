## The existing decimal_*/float_* topics cover these operators over the Decimal
## and Float domains; what has had no executable guard is the INTEGER domain
## and, above all, the four signed/unsigned MOD and DIV signatures. Go picks a
## different builtin per operand signedness
## (builtinArithmeticModIntUnsignedSignedSig and its three siblings), and the
## rows below are the ones that tell them apart:
##
##   - MOD keeps the sign of the DIVIDEND, so -13 % 11 is -2 while 13 % -11
##     is 2. A signed dividend keeps that sign even against an UNSIGNED
##     divisor (-22 % 10u is -2), which is the whole point of the
##     SignedUnsigned signature -- reading the dividend as unsigned would give
##     a huge positive remainder instead.
##   - MIN_INT64 % 3u is -2, computed without ever negating MIN_INT64 (which
##     has no positive counterpart).
##   - 2^63 as UNSIGNED % MIN_INT64 is 0: the two have the same 64-bit
##     pattern, so an implementation that compares them as signed would see
##     -2^63 % -2^63 and must still land on 0.
##   - DIV truncates toward zero, so -13 DIV 11 and 13 DIV -11 are both -1,
##     not -2: a floor division would round the wrong way for exactly the
##     mixed-sign rows.
##   - `/` always promotes to a non-integer result (11/11 is 1, 11/2 is 5.5),
##     and division or modulo by zero is NULL, never an error. On the
##     production path that result is a DECIMAL with scale 4, not the float
##     the unit test's hand-built signature returns; the value is the same and
##     the golden records the production type.
##
