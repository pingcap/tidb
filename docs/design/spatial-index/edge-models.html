Which curve is the line?

One LINESTRING(80 0, 0 80) and one POINT(45 70), in latitude–longitude. Every engine agrees on the two endpoints and disagrees about the path between them, so ST_Distance and ST_Within give different answers to the same query. Drag to rotate; scroll zooms toward whatever is under the cursor, so you can point at the two curved paths and go right down to them. Every curve is drawn true, at whatever flattening you set.

Earth shape

The geodesic is recomputed for whatever flattening you pick, so at Sphere it becomes the great circle and the gap is zero. Every metre of that gap is the flattening. Push f past WGS 84, all the way to 1/1.1, to squash the globe and watch it grow.

Nothing here is exaggerated. To see the real 1.9 km at WGS 84, use Close-up, which drops the camera to about 10 km above the point.

Watch the markers move as you drag. Geodetic latitude is defined by the surface normal, so 80N 0E names a different place on every surface: on a sphere it is one radius out, on WGS 84 about 20 km less. All three curves are drawn on whichever surface is current, so they always share their endpoints.

The point sits exactly on the great circle, so PostGIS geography returns zero and calls it a hit, while MySQL puts it 1.9 km outside the line. Distances are perpendicular to each path; along the meridian the geodesic and great-circle crossings are 7,797 m apart, and the path is running at bearing 166° there, almost due south.

Almost none of that gap is the distance formula. Andoyer and Karney disagree by 9.9 cm over 10 km as metrics, and by 2 m here. PostGIS lands 1.9 km from MySQL not because Karney is inaccurate, but because PostGIS does not use Karney for the edge: it draws a great circle and saves Karney for measuring the last hop.

Geodesics are found by integrating the surface equations along arc length and solving the initial bearing by bisection, which holds at any flattening: Vincenty’s series, the usual choice, returns nonsense past about f = 1/2 and so cannot serve this slider. At WGS 84 it agrees with Vincenty to under 2 m, and at f = 0 it reproduces the great circle exactly. The WGS 84 path is 9,810 km on an initial bearing of 99.80°, falling to 54.7° at 1/1.1, where it also crosses a vertex at 84.5°N. Crossing latitudes for Andoyer are measured on MySQL 9.7.2.
